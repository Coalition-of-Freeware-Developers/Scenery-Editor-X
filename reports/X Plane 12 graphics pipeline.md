# Rebuilding X-Plane 12's look from public techniques

X-Plane 12 as installed (the `X-Plane.exe` and shader archives are **12.4.4-b4, compiled Oct 6 2026**; the only log on disk is from **12.4.3-r2**) is a Vulkan-only, always-HDR, photometric deferred renderer: light is stored in nits, lux and candela, a simulated EV100 camera with a floor of EV 5, a GPU luminance histogram and Mertens-style exposure fusion do all of the day/night dimming, and AgX (since 12.2.0, replacing a "custom ACES blend"), an ASC-CDL grade, FXAA and spatial FSR 1 produce the display image, with no TAA and no motion vectors anywhere. Beneath that sit a five-target G-buffer (sRGB albedo, 10:10:10 material, equal-area normal, pre-exposed emissive, weather receptivity), 2.5D tiled lighting with overflow "spill" volumes, up to 8 GGX-filtered cube probes with a split-sum table that matches height-correlated Smith, 1 to 4 sun shadow cascades plus up to 9 temporally filtered cloud-shadow cascades, AMD CACAO, Hi-Z screen-space reflections, a Hillaire-2020-style atmosphere with froxel aerial perspective and froxel volumetric fog, SDF-accelerated ray-marched clouds at 20 to 50% resolution with temporal upscaling, a JONSWAP/TMA FFT ocean with four cascades and a slope-variance volume, and a deferred weather pass that rewrites albedo, gloss and normals for rain, puddles, snow and ice. Every subsystem maps to a published technique, and nearly all have permissively licensed references (MIT Hillaire sky, Apache-2.0 Khronos IBL sampler and PBR Neutral, MIT FidelityFX CACAO and SSSR, MIT GodotOceanWaves, public-domain Wronski exposure fusion; clouds and froxel fog have only talks and unverified-licence ports), so a near 1:1 emulation in Scenery Editor X (SEDX) is mostly an integration and calibration job rather than a research one. The hard parts are the authoring contract, since much of the material vocabulary in shipped 12.4 scenery (`SUPER_ROUGHNESS`, `WEATHER` masks, `SNOW_ALBEDO_LUMA`, `SEASONAL`, `BLEND_HASH`, `NORMAL_TRANSLUCENCY`) is undocumented, and the calibration, since the defaults of roughly 1,000 private rendering art controls (tone-curve mode, CDL grade, sky-condition ratios, exposure curves) exist only inside the running sim; a DataRefTool dump, RenderDoc captures of X-Plane and controlled test scenery can close most of those gaps. Laminar's shaders, SPIR-V/MSL archives and lookup tables are proprietary: this report uses them only as a behavioral reference for interfaces, formats and data flow, and SEDX must implement every pass from published sources, never by copying or transliterating Laminar's code.

## A Vulkan-only deferred renderer built around a five-target G-buffer

Laminar's own statements set the frame. X-Plane 12 renders through Vulkan or Metal "always", and OpenGL survives only for plugins ([Supnik, Early Access](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)). Deferred shading is the only path, and "real MSAA" works inside it ([Dev Update, March 2022](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)). Since 12.06 the frame has been a render node graph instead of hand-coded passes. That lets transient VRAM be reused within a frame and makes barriers static, and Sidney Just noted the conversion needed zero shader changes ([12.06 post](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)). The installed binary carries the strings `12.4.4-b4` and "Compiled on Oct 6 2026", while the log header reads 12.4.3-r2, build 124311 ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe); [Log.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Log.txt)). Everything below therefore describes the 12.4.4 beta shaders, and no 12.4.3-to-12.4.4 diff is possible. The shader folder holds **97 pass families**. They are GLSL compiled with glslang (debug names intact), plus SPIRV-Cross Metal versions. The `terrain` uber-shader alone declares 829,440 permutation slots, which collapse to 4,614 unique modules ([shaders/bin](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/)). Several plumbing choices are visible: descriptor sets are grouped by update frequency and bound with push descriptors, no push constants are used, stereo goes through layered 2D-array targets with `gl_Layer` instead of multiview, and the engine has native reverse-Z and an async compute queue ([Log.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Log.txt); [SPIR-V archives](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/)). SEDX does not need to copy any of that plumbing. It does need the data contract, and the contract starts with the G-buffer.

The world G-buffer has **five colour targets plus depth/stencil**, named `gbuf-albedo`, `gbuf-material`, `gbuf-normal`, `gbuf-emissive` and `gbuf-weather` in the executable ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). Their encodings come from three places: what the terrain writer outputs, what the deferred lighting pass decodes, and the storage views the weather pass edits in place ([terrain MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/terrain.xsa); [deferred_gbuf MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/deferred_gbuf.xsa); [weather_apply_compute](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/weather_apply_compute.xsa)).

| MRT | Name | Storage (observed or inferred) | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | gbuf-albedo | RGBA8, sRGB-encoded | albedo × coverage, A = coverage |
| 1 | gbuf-material | RGB10A2 (aliased as R32UI) | R = per-material lighting factor (terrain writes 1.0); G = ½ + ½·s, where positive s is gloss; B = ½ + ½·metal − ½·(second property); A = coverage |
| 2 | gbuf-normal | RG16F (aliased as R32UI) | eye-space normal, Lambert azimuthal equal-area encoded |
| 3 | gbuf-emissive | format not visible (RGBA16F or R11G11B10F plausible) | emissive × exposure scale |
| 4 | gbuf-weather | format not visible | rain, puddle and snow receptivity (snow signed) |
| depth | gbuf-depth | reverse-Z, probably D32 with stencil | stencil appears to carry classification bits (water, cockpit, FXAA mask) |

Every output is premultiplied by coverage × LOD fade, which is what lets decals and blended materials blend into the G-buffer. Under MSAA, 432 terrain modules write their alpha-to-coverage mask in the shader. An `msaa_categorize` pass discards pixels whose samples all share one normal, so per-sample shading runs only on edges ([msaa_categorize MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/msaa_categorize.xsa)). Since 12.1.0 the MSAA resolve is scene-referred, and FXAA always runs alongside MSAA ([12.1.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/)). **There is no motion-vector target.** The `gbuffer_vel` binding that SSR reads is a uint classification mask. Laminar staff have said that DLSS, TAA and FSR 2 are ruled out because the "fat g-buffer" has no motion vectors ([March 2022 update](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/); [Early Access](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)). That simplifies SEDX's job: temporal smearing is not part of the reference look, so a faithful emulation must not add TAA.

The executable's render-graph labels (from `2_plot_frame_graph.cpp` and dozens of `OGL_build_*_graph` builders), together with which pass produces and which pass consumes each binding, give one consistent order. The order is inferred from string order and data flow, not captured, and the placement of the sky passes is the least certain step ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)).

The pass set changed a lot between versions. A 12.06 beta log lists 81 families, including a custom `deferred_ssao` + `ssao_blur`, a screen-space `csm_resolve`, per-light `lit` passes and `stars` ([12.06 log](file:///G:/Repositorys/Coalition-of-Freeware-Developers/Scenery-Editor-X/XP12_Vulkan_Log.txt)). 12.4.4-b4 has replaced those with CACAO, folded the shadow resolve into deferred lighting, and switched to tiled lighting with spill volumes. It has also added exposure fusion, compute bloom, FidelityFX DoF, froxel volumetric fog and variable-rate shading ([shaders/bin](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/)). The look-defining release was 12.2.0 (March to June 2025). It brought AgX, exposure fusion, the sky recalibration and the cloud overhaul. No tone-mapper change appears in the 12.3 to 12.4.4 notes ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)). **SEDX should target the post-12.2 look**, and treat anything tuned against 12.0 or 12.1 screenshots as stale.

## Nits in, EV100 out: the camera does all the dimming

Ben Supnik's 2021 explainer is still the best statement of intent. The pipeline is "entirely HDR, from start to finish" in 16-bit float, and luminance is real-world cd/m². Reference levels run from **building floodlights at about 2 nt, clear sky about 7,000, clouds about 10,000 and a sunset sun about 600,000, up to a noon sun of 1.6 billion nits**. A simulated camera's exposure, set by the art team by time of day and weather and then modulated by auto-exposure, maps that range to the display ([Photometric Lighting](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)). The working gamut stays sRGB, with albedos authored in sRGB, and UV/IR are not modelled ([same post](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)). DataRefs.txt states the consequence: "no light source is _ever_ dimmed - the camera itself changes exposure" ([DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)). The art-control namespace carries the same units: sun and moon illuminance in lux (`lighting/E_sun_lx`, `E_moon_lx`), reference luminances in nits (`scenery_ref_nts`, `moon/nits`), and a `photometric/light_storage_scale` ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). A community script records the stock 12.0x sun as 115,000 lx and the EV100 range as 5 to 15. These values predate the 12.2 recalibration, so treat them as starting points only ([rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/FlyWithLua/Scripts/rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua)).

There is a storage consequence too. A 1.6-billion-nit sun cannot fit in fp16, whose maximum is 65,504. The terrain writer multiplies emissive by `u_exposure_scale.y` before storing it ([terrain MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/terrain.xsa)), and a dedicated `fix_hdr` pass clamps any ±inf in the HDR target to ±65,504 before post-processing ([fix_hdr MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/fix_hdr.xsa)). The inference is that X-Plane stores **pre-exposed** radiance in its RGBA16F targets. SEDX should do the same: carry scene values in lux, nits and candela in its constants, apply the current exposure when writing to fp16, and keep a guard pass for infinities.

### Exposure is a metered EV100 camera with a hard floor

Ben stated in 2023 that X-Plane's minimum exposure is **EV100 = 5**, which is why real moonlight is too weak to register and why moonlight on the ground was deferred ([12.06 post](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)). The controls are `photometric/ev100`, `ev100_min`, `ev100_max`, `ev100_mid`, `ev100_mtr`, `ev100_bias`, `K` and `speed`, plus `autoexposure/` gains, trims and null bands and `exposure/speed_up` / `speed_down` ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The metering pass `lumi_histo` builds a 256-bin histogram with spot-metering parameters (`u_spot_center`, `u_spot_radius`) and a temporal blend speed, and the result is read back to the CPU. That readback path dates from a 12.0 RC change that moved "tone mapping from GPU blending to CPU readback" ([lumi_histo](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/lumi_histo.xsa); [12.00 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes/)). The art-directed part lives in per-sky-condition `skyc/tone_ratio_*` multipliers and an internal `ev100_bias` table. A community script approximates that table as +3.5 EV at EV 5, +3.0 at EV 8 and 0 at EV 11 to 12 ([rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/FlyWithLua/Scripts/rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua)). The closest published recipe is Opsenica's histogram auto-exposure: 256 log bins, shared-memory atomics, and EV100 = log2(L_avg·S/K) with S = 100 and K = 12.5. His sample states no licence, so use it as a formula reference ([bruop](https://bruop.github.io/exposure/)).

### Exposure fusion fixed the dark cockpit by fusing luminance only

Laminar's position from 2021 to 2025 was consistent: the lighting is physically right, and the single global exposure is the perceptual weak point. At FSExpo 2024 it blamed dark cockpits on "the difference in exposure between the very bright exterior and the relatively dark interior" ([fsnews.eu](https://fsnews.eu/x-plane-12-to-receive-new-weather-radar-dark-cockpit-fix-and-more/)). 12.2.0 shipped **exposure fusion**, which "balances dark and bright areas within the same frame" ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)). Marco Auer compared it to a phone camera that takes several exposures and merges them ([What's new in 12.2.0](https://www.x-plane.com/2025/04/whats-new-in-x-plane-12-2-0/)). The pass binds luma, weight and accumulation stacks, takes `u_master_exposure`, `u_shadow_gain` and `u_sigma2` parameters, and its labels include "EF gauss accum" and "EF laplac accum". Only luminance is fused, and the result is a `tex_lumi_guide` that the tonemap pass uses to rescale colour ([exposure_fusion](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/exposure_fusion.xsa)). That matches Mertens et al.'s exposure fusion ([Mertens 2007](https://www.jankautz.com/publications/exposure_fusion.pdf)) as adapted for real time by Bart Wronski. Wronski uses synthetic exposures, only the Gaussian "well-exposedness" weight, Laplacians as differences of Gaussian mips, a coarsest level around mip 5 to 6, and processing at quarter resolution with guided upsampling, all in public-domain demo code ([Wronski 2022](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)). X-Plane's own knobs (`exposure_fusion/dis_near`, `dis_far`, `max_mip`, `shadows`, `sigma`, `hq_blur`, plus `autoexposure/adaptive_ef_*`) map cleanly onto Wronski's exposure, shadows, coarsest mip and sigma ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). For a scenery editor viewed mostly from outside, this pass matters less than for cockpit views, but it is part of the look.

### AgX plus an ASC-CDL grade replaced the ACES blend in 12.2

12.2.0 "switched from our custom ACES blend to AgX, improving overall color accuracy, especially in bright and saturated scenes" ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)). Laminar warned authors that "colors will shift in appearance" and that colours "desaturate under direct illumination" ([SDK News 12.2.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2025/04/12-2-0-chockingly-good-fun/)). The older blend was configurable through a `tonemap/blend` control that a 12.06-era script describes as "a mix of ACESFilmic and Uncharted2". That control, together with `tonemap/white_balance_k` and `tonemap/desaturate`, no longer exists in 12.4.4-b4 ([rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/FlyWithLua/Scripts/rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua)). The current tonemap uniform block is `u_tonemap_mode`, `u_display_maximum`, `u_mid_gray`, a working-colour-space matrix and its inverse, `u_wcs_lumi`, ASC-CDL `slope/offset/power/saturation`, `u_output_gain`, `u_output_gamma` and `u_exposure_rat` ([hdr](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/hdr.xsa)). The art controls add `color_management/compression` and `abney_correction` ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The display transform is therefore: inset into a working space, AgX sigmoid, gamut compression with a hue (Abney) correction, a per-channel CDL, then gain, gamma and the `B8G8R8A8_SRGB` swapchain ([Log.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Log.txt)). AgX's structure is public (inset matrix, log2 encoding, sigmoid, outset matrix, display EOTF). Blender's variant differs from Sobotka's original, and the multi-language AgXc ports have an unverified licence ([AgXc](https://www.github.com/MrLixm/AgXc)). Khronos PBR Neutral (Apache-2.0, about 13 lines of GLSL) is the safe fallback curve until the X-Plane variant is fitted ([KhronosGroup/ToneMapping](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping)).

The remaining post stages are conservative. Bloom is a compute "rip" (threshold, `u_cut_lo_hi`) followed by separable blur with 15 tap gains over a configurable mip range ([bloom](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/bloom.xsa)). Laminar tuned it to "mimic the way the _eye_ perceives the bloom, not a camera", made it visible in reflections, and reduced it in 12.2.0, which also added a high-altitude sun-glare pass ([A Few Notes on 12.1.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/02/a-few-notes-on-12-1-0-for-developers/); [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)). Depth of field is AMD FidelityFX DoF, driven by `dof/fnumber`, `focus_distance` and `sensor_size`. Those controls and `photometric/ev100` are the only three art controls stored as full dataref strings, next to the screenshot tool ([depth_of_field](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/depth_of_field.xsa); [X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). Upscaling is **FSR 1**: one colour input, EASU constants, a 64-thread workgroup, no depth or motion inputs, and mip biases of −0.38, −0.58, −0.79 and −1.0 for the four modes ([fsr](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/fsr.xsa); [settings.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/settings.txt)). The binary contains no art controls for film grain, vignette, chromatic aberration, lens flare or LUT grading, so a faithful emulation adds none of them ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)).

## Materials decode to GGX metalness with a 1/32 roughness floor

Laminar documents the model as an industry-standard metalness workflow matching Disney/UE4, Substance Painter 2 and Blender's Principled BSDF. Metalness and F0 share **one channel**: values from 0 to 0.08 act as both metalness and F0, while anything above 0.08 counts as metal with the dielectric F0 clamped to 0.08 ([XP12 material model](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/the-x-plane-12-material-model)). Without `NORMAL_METALNESS`, default F0 follows a priority list: 0.06 for `BLEND_GLASS` in aircraft glass modes, 0.0 for translucency-mode normal maps, 0.04 for any other normal map or for gloss > 0, and 0.0 at default maximum roughness ([same](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/the-x-plane-12-material-model)). Gloss rather than roughness is authored, in normal-map alpha, with 1.0 glossiest, and every channel is 8-bit ([XP11 material model](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/x-plane-11-material-model/)). XP12 also stopped adding XP11's "extra specularity" to intermediate metals (F0 about 0.25 to 0.75), so XP11-tuned metals look brighter and washed out in XP12. It added an optional translucency mode for vegetation, and it does not offer baked AO on OBJs ([XP12 material model](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/the-x-plane-12-material-model)).

Laminar publishes no BRDF equations, but the deferred pass makes the decode observable ([deferred_gbuf MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/deferred_gbuf.xsa)). Gloss is max(0, 2G − 1), and perceptual roughness is **1 − 0.96875 · gloss**, which puts the smoothest surface at 1/32 rather than 0. Metalness is clamp(2B − 1, 0, 1). The split-sum table is sampled at (N·V, perceptual roughness). Specular IBL reads the probe array at LOD = roughness · 5 + a bias, and diffuse IBL reads the same array at LOD 6. The 524,288-byte `Resources/brdf_lookup` decodes cleanly only as a 256 × 256 RG32F table. Against a 1,024-sample importance-sampled integral it matches **GGX with α = roughness², height-correlated Smith visibility and Schlick Fresnel**. It does not match UE4's Schlick-G (k = α/2) or α = roughness ([brdf_lookup](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/brdf_lookup)). That pins SEDX's BRDF choice: GGX, height-correlated Smith, α = r². SEDX should generate its own DFG table at startup with Filament's formulation, which is a 2D table indexed by (N·V, perceptual roughness) in RG16F ([Filament](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)), on top of Karis's split-sum ([Karis 2013](https://cgvr.informatik.uni-bremen.de/teaching/cg_literatur/Brian%20Karis%20-%20Real%20Shading%20in%20Unreal%20Engine%204,%202013%20Siggraph%20Course.pdf)). Whether X-Plane applies multiple-scattering energy compensation is not visible, so leave Filament's compensation term switchable.

### Eight probes, cosine irradiance in mip 6, refreshed as the sun moves

The IBL block holds up to **8 cube probes** with separate diffuse and specular counts and per-probe eye-to-probe transforms, sampled from a `textureCubeArray` ([deferred_gbuf](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/deferred_gbuf.xsa)). `cube_filter_compute` writes six storage cube faces per mip. It has a GGX mode and a cosine mode (`USE_COS`), filter floors expressed in nits (`ibl/ggx/floors_nts`, `ibl/cos/floors_nts`) and a `u_filter_log_omega_s` term that suggests filtered importance sampling ([cube_filter_compute](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/cube_filter_compute.xsa)). The log shows the probe context at 256 × 256 ([Log.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Log.txt)). Art controls cover update mode and period, recalculation when the sun moves (`cube/recalc_degs_cos`), probe LOD biases for forests and objects, and cloud and scattering resolution inside the probe render. There are "External Cubemap" and "Internal Cubemap" probes for aircraft interiors ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). Since 12.2, ambient light "updates more often and is calculated separately for the interior and exterior of the aircraft" ([What's new in 12.2.0](https://www.x-plane.com/2025/04/whats-new-in-x-plane-12-2-0/)). The inferred mip layout is mip 0 as the source, mips 1 to 5 GGX-prefiltered at roughness·5, and mip 6 cosine irradiance. The Khronos glTF-IBL-Sampler (Apache-2.0, Vulkan/GLSL, Lambertian and GGX filters plus a BRDF LUT) is the right reference to turn into an amortized runtime compute prefilter ([glTF-IBL-Sampler](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-IBL-Sampler)).

### SSR and CACAO are screen-space layers over the probes

`ssr_deferred` is a Hi-Z compute march over the G-buffer. It reads the previous frame's lit HDR as a history mip tree, reprojects with camera matrices alone (`u_reproj`, `u_local_reproj`), skips pixels below `u_ssr_min_gloss`, and can trace from water as a second source. `ssr_mesh` handles cockpit glass ([ssr_deferred](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/ssr_deferred.xsa)). FidelityFX SSSR 1.5 (MIT, with HLSL and GLSL/Vulkan) has the canonical structure: a 7-mip SPD depth pyramid, tile classification by roughness, hierarchical intersection, a denoiser and a cube-map fallback. It expects motion vectors, though, so SEDX must substitute matrix reprojection to match X-Plane ([SSSR manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/stochastic-screen-space-reflections/)). Ambient occlusion is **AMD FidelityFX CACAO**. The evidence is the `FFX_CACAO_*` shared memory, the deinterleave, importance-map and bilateral-upscale stages, and the "lowest-low-medium-high-highest" enum ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). X-Plane's `ssao/` controls mirror CACAO's parameter list (radius, shadow multiplier, power and clamp, horizon threshold, sharpness, blur passes), and it adds interior and exterior strength curves of its own ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). CACAO 1.4 is MIT and has GLSL in FidelityFX SDK 1.1.4, but AMD now marks all SDK 1 effects deprecated. XeGTAO (MIT, archived) is the alternative if bent normals are wanted ([CACAO manual](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/combined-adaptive-compute-ambient-occlusion/); [FidelityFX SDK releases](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/releases); [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO)). How X-Plane applies AO to direct versus indirect light is not decoded. Filament's specular-occlusion formula is the standard choice for indirect specular ([Filament](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)).

### Shadows: one to four sun cascades, three in the cockpit, plus cloud shadows

Scenery shadows are cascaded shadow maps in a `texture2DArrayShadow` holding four layers. Split placement uses a practical-split λ (`shadow/csm/lambda`), with constant, slope and normal biases and a soft (PCF-style) mode resolved inside deferred lighting. There is no separate shadow shader family, which suggests depth-only reuse of the scene pipelines ([deferred_gbuf](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/deferred_gbuf.xsa); [X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). Laminar called the maps "currently too sharp" in April 2025 ([SDK News 12.2.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2025/04/12-2-0-chockingly-good-fun/)). No contact-shadow control exists. Beyond dynamic range, scenery falls back to baked shadow in `TEXTURE_CONTROL` alpha ([Standard Shading Options](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/standard-shading-options-for-x-plane-10-scenery/)). For SEDX, Pettineo's stable-cascade practice (sphere fit, texel snapping, GatherCmp PCF, cascade blending) is the reference. His sample states no licence, so read it rather than vendor it ([MJP shadows](https://therealmjp.github.io/posts/shadow-maps/)). The settings file gives quality anchors that SEDX can reuse directly as presets ([settings.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/settings.txt)):

| UI level | 3-D shadows: exterior cascades / far limit / map size / fade | SSAO: CACAO level / resolution | Clouds: scale / max px / hi-noise km / shadow steps | FSR: mode / mip bias |

| 0 | 1 / 500 m / 2048 / 0.999 (aircraft only) | off | 0.20 / 980 / 20 / 3 (no colour) | off |

Interior (cockpit) cascades stay at 3 at every level. The RTX 3090 profile on this machine runs shadows 2, clouds 2, SSAO 2, 2× MSAA, FXAA on and FSR off ([X-Plane.prf](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Output/preferences/X-Plane.prf)).

### Night lighting is tiled, photometric and calibrated to FAA spec sheets

Airport light intensities are "calibrated using spec sheets from the FAA" ([March 2022 update](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)). In the shipped `lights.txt`, the billboard size column became **intensity in candela** (`375cd` taillights, `20000cd` headlight spills, `95000cd` beacons), and V12 has "only one billboard ... tinted with same RGB values of the spill" ([lights.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/world/lites/lights.txt)). The catalogue holds 494 light names in 872 overload records and 97 parameter signatures. New `_pm` lights take an `INTENSITY` parameter, and billboards come from one 16 × 8-cell atlas ([lights.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/world/lites/lights.txt)). On the GPU, `light_transform` culls the light list. `light_tile` then bins lights against Hi-Z min/max into screen tiles with **two depth slices per tile and up to 512 lights per tile**. Lights that overflow are marked "spilled", compacted by `light_spill_compact` into interior and exterior lists, and drawn as additive light volumes through two indirect commands ([light_tile](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/light_tile.xsa); [light_spill_compact](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/light_spill_compact.xsa)). Billboards remain forward-drawn, fogged per vertex by aerial perspective, clouds and volumetric fog ([light](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/light.xsa)). 12.2.0 optimized "light rendering" (tiled lighting), and 12.4.4 added a "hybrid tiled lighting system" for dense airports ([SDK News 12.2.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2025/04/12-2-0-chockingly-good-fun/); [12.4.4 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-4-release-notes/)). Forward+ (Harada 2012) and clustered shading (Olsson 2012) are the published bases, and the Vulkan tutorial's Forward+ chapter is a direct reference ([Forward+](https://diglib.eg.org/items/1db2c4c6-dcab-42ea-8c0a-6805d781759e/full); [Olsson 2012](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/161725); [Vulkan Forward+](https://docs.vulkan.org/tutorial/latest/Building_a_Simple_Engine/Advanced_Topics/ForwardPlus_Rendering.html)). Emissive textures follow the same photometry. The OBJ directive `GLOBAL_luminance` sets the LIT peak in nits (clamped at 65,530; shipped values 1,000, 60, 100 and 150), and terrain uses an undocumented `LUMINANCE 600` with `LIT_FADE_PARAMS` ([OBJ8 spec](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/obj8-file-format-specification/); [urban_tmp_wet1.ter](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/1000%20world%20terrain/terrain12/urban_tmp_wet1.ter)). City lights do not light clouds; Laminar tied that to a future light-pollution map ([12.06 post](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)).

## Sky, haze, clouds and water share one participating medium

### The atmosphere is Hillaire 2020 with RGB transmittance volumes and a fog layer

Laminar has never named its atmosphere paper. It has said that scattering for scenery is "pre-calculated in a special 'froxel cache'", that clouds use the same cache, and that the model includes ozone absorption ([Free Form Friday](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/); [Early Access](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)). 12.2.0 "recalibrated Rayleigh scattering, ozone coefficients, Mie scattering, and multiple scattering" and made "clouds now cast visible shadows on the atmosphere" ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)). The bindings match Hillaire's EGSR 2020 method stage for stage ([Hillaire 2020](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14050)). `scatter_compute_t` writes an rgba16f transmittance LUT whose texels are (view-zenith, altitude) pairs, integrating Rayleigh, Mie, ozone and fog density over **40 steps**, the same fixed count Hillaire's demo uses ([scatter_compute_t MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/scatter_compute_t.xsa); [RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/master/Resources/RenderSkyRayMarching.hlsl)). `scatter_compute_multi` then writes the multi-scattering LUT, and `scatter_compute_skydome` writes sky-view, skydome and "Sky Ambient" images, with a variant that samples the cloud-shadow cascades. Finally `scatter_compute_scattering` writes **two 3D r11g11b10f volumes, in-scatter and out-scatter**. Ten pass families consume those volumes, among them deferred lighting, forward terrain, both cloud renderers, volumetric fog, particles, the ocean, light billboards and the orbital planet ([scatter_compute_skydome](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/scatter_compute_skydome.xsa); [scatter_compute_scattering](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/scatter_compute_scattering.xsa)). X-Plane departs from Hillaire's paper in three visible ways. It stores RGB transmittance instead of Hillaire's greyscale alpha. Its medium has a fourth, ground-fog layer (`u_fog_ele`, `u_scatter_f`, `u_absorb_f`, `atmo/fog_albedo`). Its Mie phase is tabulated (`tex_mie_phase` with `u_mie_decode`) next to a `scattering/g_cornette_shanks` control, while Hillaire's demo evaluates Cornette–Shanks analytically ([scatter_compute_t](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/scatter_compute_t.xsa); [RenderSkyCommon.hlsl](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere/blob/master/Resources/RenderSkyCommon.hlsl)). Baking cloud shadows into the sky-view and aerial-perspective LUTs is exactly the shortcut Hillaire proposes ([Hillaire slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)). A separate `atmosphere` family carries Bruneton 2017's exact texture and uniform names, but no other pass samples it, so it is vestigial or a reference path ([atmosphere](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/atmosphere.xsa)).

The MIT-licensed `sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere` HLSL ports almost line for line to Slang. Its demo constants (256 × 64 transmittance, 192 × 108 sky-view with non-linear zenith mapping, 32 aerial-perspective slices of 4 km) are sensible defaults until X-Plane's runtime resolutions are captured ([sebh repo](https://github.com/sebh/UnrealEngineSkyAtmosphere); [Hillaire slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)). Bruneton's BSD code remains the accuracy reference ([ebruneton](https://github.com/ebruneton/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering)). The weather side is driven by 11 sky-condition presets (`clean, hazy, foggy, sockd, ocast, strat, snowy, mount, hialt, orbit, final`), each with ambient, direct, tone, Rayleigh, Mie and shadow ratios, plus `atmo/rayleigh_*` and `atmo/ozone_*` ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The stock pre-12.2 ozone values were 0.65 / 1.881 / 0.085, with a tent centred at 25 km and 30 km wide ([rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/FlyWithLua/Scripts/rxp-xp12-enhancer.lua)). The dome adds the sun disc with glare falloff, and since 12.2 sun and moon have real-world size and brightness and the moon shifts colour near the horizon ([dome](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/dome.xsa); [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)).

### Visibility is calibrated to a just-visible monolith and confined to a surface layer

Low visibility is calibrated so a runway "monolith" is "just barely visible" at the set distance ([March 2022 update](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)). That is a contrast-threshold definition, so SEDX can solve for the fog-layer extinction that produces it. Since 12.4.4 beta 1, visibility "fades from the ground-reported value to clear-air visibility over a physically derived altitude band", which keeps fog, mist and haze in a shallow surface layer ([12.4.4 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-4-release-notes/)). Near the camera, a froxel `volumetric_fog` pass builds density and then a temporally accumulated rgba16f scattering volume. Its inputs include height-fog density, elevation and width, separate rain albedo and scatter, cloud precipitation, cloud shadows, sky ambient and volumetric lights. `volumetric_apply` composites it with the clouds ([volumetric_fog](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/volumetric_fog.xsa)). That is Wronski's 2014 froxel design, unified with lights and shadows as in Hillaire's 2015 Frostbite work ([Wronski GDC 2014](https://bartwronski.com/2014/03/09/my-upcoming-gdc-2014-presentation/); [Frostbite volumetrics](https://www.ea.com/news/physically-based-unified-volumetric-rendering-in-frostbite)). It can share its froxel parameterization with the aerial-perspective volume. No canonical permissive froxel-fog repository was identified, so this pass is written from the talks.

### Clouds are SDF-accelerated ray marches at a fraction of screen resolution

Ben called clouds "probably the single most expensive part of the renderer" ([Early Access](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)). The pipeline starts from a weather SSBO that `cloud_update` turns into 3D coverage (r16ui) and properties (rgba8) volumes. `cloud_minify` builds a 3D signed distance field by jump flooding (an alpha note on "Cloud SDF broken for thin cumulus" confirms the SDF). Ray marching then samples low- and high-frequency 3D noise, a 2D cirrus noise, sky ambient, transmittance, the scattering volumes and cloud shadows. It uses segment marching, an SDF skip rate, a phase g, a multi-scatter length and temporal jitter. The march writes rgba16f colour and rg16f depth, and `cloud_upscale` reprojects them against history with depth-change and brightness clips ([cloud_render_compute](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/cloud_render_compute.xsa); [cloud_upscale](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/cloud_upscale.xsa); [Alpha notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes-alphas/)). At cloud quality 2 the buffer is half resolution (960 × 540 for a 1080p window) ([Log.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Log.txt)). 12.2.0 added inter-layer and self-shadowing, a new shaping algorithm, shadows that respect density and Earth curvature, and fully 3D lighting within 1 km of the camera. Marco Auer described the lighting result as unlit edges going dark and lit edges going white ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/); [What's new in 12.2.0](https://www.x-plane.com/2025/04/whats-new-in-x-plane-12-2-0/)). Cloud shadows are a temporally filtered rgba16f map with jitter and reprojection. Receivers sample it as a 2D array of up to **9 cascades**, it shadows water, and since 12.1 it lives in world space "akin to regular shadows" ([cloud_shadows](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/cloud_shadows.xsa); [12.1.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/)). Guerrilla's Nubis talks are the published lineage ([Nubis Evolved](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/nubis-evolved)). Hillaire's Beer shadow map is the reference for long-distance cloud transmittance ([Hillaire slides](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2020-shading-course/hillaire/s2020_pbs_hillaire_slides.pdf)). The Vulkan student ports (Meteoros, Frostnova) have unverified licences and should be treated as reading material ([Meteoros](https://github.com/AmanSachan1/Meteoros); [Frostnova](https://github.com/YueZhang1027/CIS5650-Final-Project-Frostnova)).

### Water is a JONSWAP/TMA FFT ocean on a projected grid, coloured per 1° tile

`ocean_spectrum` takes JONSWAP-style peak frequency and γ, swell, spreading blend, short-wave fade and depth. Those parameters describe a TMA directional spectrum, which `compute_fft` turns into four cascades (`water/cascade_0..3_scale`). `ocean_variance` builds a 3D rg16f slope-variance table, and `ocean_mesh_*` bakes a GPU projected-grid mesh drawn indirectly ([ocean_spectrum](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/ocean_spectrum.xsa); [X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). Shading reads water normal, meta and colour targets, the skydome cube, SSR, the variance volume, the scattering volumes and both shadow types. It takes specular F0 and α², foam parameters and a turbidity term ([ocean_shading](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/ocean_shading.xsa)). The variance table is the Bruneton–Neyret–Holzschuch 2010 idea of folding sub-pixel wave slopes into the BRDF so distant water does not sparkle ([Bruneton 2010](https://evasion.inrialpes.fr/Publications/2010/BNH10)). Tessendorf and Horvath 2015 supply the spectrum math ([Tessendorf](https://jtessen.people.clemson.edu/reports/papers_files/waterslides2001.pdf); [Horvath 2015](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F2791261.2791267)), and the MIT GodotOceanWaves implements TMA, spreading plus swell, Stockham FFT cascades and Jacobian foam ([GodotOceanWaves](https://github.com/2Retr0/GodotOceanWaves)). On the authoring side, DSF water triangles carry a fetch ratio (1 = open ocean, 0 = pond) and a depth value. XP12 treats the depth as an interpolation ratio between ground elevation and the required `sea_level` bathymetry raster ([DSF Usage](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/dsf-usage-in-x-plane/)). Users set only wave height and direction, and wave length and speed are derived and not writable since v12 ([DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)). Water colour comes from 15,281 per-1°-tile 512² RGBA PNGs, with `any.png` (13, 32, 51, 191) as the fallback. Alpha is plausibly clarity ([bitmaps/world/water](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/world/water/)). Since 12.1.0, "Water color and clarity for default water depends on location" ([12.1.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/)).

## Weather and seasons edit the G-buffer and swap library assets

### One deferred pass applies rain, puddles, snow and ice after the G-buffer is written

Petr and Sidney's "weather surface shader" makes pavement wet, changes shading "once the water forms a real puddle", and lets reflections on rough wet materials change with angle ([Free Form Friday](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/)). In 12.4.4-b4 this is `weather_apply_compute`. It edits albedo (rgba8, re-encoded through sRGB in the shader), material (10:10:10 in R32UI) and normal (half2 in R32UI) **in place**, and a copy-style raster fallback exists ([weather_apply_compute](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/weather_apply_compute.xsa)). The pass reads the fifth MRT, depth, a rain-area noise texture, a `tex_droplets` ripple texture (produced by a `ripple` compute pass), and global snow and ice layers (`snow_ALB/NML/DCL`, `ice_ALB/NML/DCL` in `bitmaps/world/weather`). It also reads a **world-projected `tex_snow_coverage`** map, which the weather engine produces as `ground_snow_coverage` ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). In the Metal build, the weather vector's x component drives rain darkening and gloss and roughness changes through smoothsteps around global levels. The y component drives puddles, blending normals toward up plus ripples and raising gloss. The signed z component drives snow, combined with the coverage map, a slope test against the up normal and noise ([weather_apply_compute MSL](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/msl/weather_apply_compute.xsa)). The tuning controls (`twxr/rain_darkening_*`, `rain_darken_albedo_rat`, `rain_darken_gloss_rat`, `snow/slope_min|max`, `ice/decal*`, `*_area_scale|width`) sit next to the live state (`wxr/rain_now`, `puddles_now`, `snow_now`, `ice_now`) and to five-point in/out keyframe curves for each effect (`hack/petr/wxr/kf/*`) ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The weather layers are themselves PBR materials in the metalness packing: snow normals have B = 10/255 (F0 ≈ 0.04) and A = 153 (gloss 0.6), and ice has B = 13 and A = 192 ([bitmaps/world/weather](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/world/weather/)).

This structure settles a design choice. The reference notes suggested Lagarde's persistent flood maps, but X-Plane's pass has **no persistent world-space wetness texture**. Wetness is global state × per-material receptivity × noise, and only snow has a world map. For 1:1, SEDX should therefore take Lagarde's formulas for the look and X-Plane's structure for the state. The formulas are porosity-driven darkening `lerp(1, 0.2, porosity)`, gloss pushed toward water, puddles flattened to an up normal with water F0 0.02, and four time-shifted ripple layers ([Lagarde 3b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/04/14/water-drop-3b-physically-based-wet-surfaces/); [Lagarde 2b](https://seblagarde.wordpress.com/2013/01/03/water-drop-2b-dynamic-rain-and-its-effects/)). Snow follows the shipped-game recipe of an up-component blend with a top-down coverage map ([Company of Heroes 2](https://www.slideshare.net/proyZ/daniel-barrero-coh2renderingtech)). The public state is coarse. `sim/weather/region/runway_friction` folds everything into dry 0, wet 1–3, puddly 4–6, snowy 7–9, icy 10–12 and snowy/icy 13–15, alongside `snow_cover` and `rain_percent` ([DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)). Until 12.4.4, ground snow came from weather settings. 12.4.4 beta 1 uses GRIB data to make snow "persistent and regionalized", horizontal only, and not freezing water bodies ([12.4.4 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-4-release-notes/)). The install also ships twelve monthly 0.1° MODIS-style snow-cover maps, which are plausibly a climatological fallback ([Snow Cover](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/Snow%20Cover/)). Weather has been applied to main roads since 12.2.0, and hangar interiors stopped collecting snow in 12.3.0 ([12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/); [12.3.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-3-0-release-notes/)). The `rain_*` passes are a separate windshield simulation (droplets, wipers, heaters, glass ice with CPU readback) and do not touch scenery.

### Seasons are library swaps plus a next-season albedo blend

Laminar's 2021 pitch was continuous seasons from climate and biome data, with bare winter trees. Austin Meyer said the shaders interpolate "between time", "based on latitude" ([Threshold, FSExpo 2021](https://thresholdx.net/xp12fs/); [FSElite previews](https://fselite.net/content/new-previews-of-the-next-generation-of-x-plane/)). The shipped mechanism has three layers. First, `library.txt` gained `EXPORT_SEASON`, `EXPORT_EXCLUDE_SEASON`, `EXPORT_EXTEND_SEASON` and `EXPORT_RATIO_SEASON`, each taking comma lists of `spr sum fal win`. Exports must cover all four seasons and cannot be mixed with legacy exports for one virtual path ([library.txt spec](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/library-library-txt-file-format-specification/)). Second, DSF files carry eight rasters (`spr1/spr2` … `win1/win2`) of day-of-year ranges, which may wrap the year, and "seasonal art is interpolated between ranges" ([DSF Usage](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/dsf-usage-in-x-plane/)). Third, forest shaders name a `SEASONAL` texture that is the next season's albedo, in a cycle: spring → summer → fall → winter → spring ([win/AG_temp_1.for](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/1200%20forests/win/AG_temp_1.for)).

Usage differs by asset type. The default libraries have 1,804 `EXPORT_SEASON` and 9,980 `EXPORT_EXCLUDE_SEASON` lines. Terrain has 2 or 3 states (spring shares summer's art) and swaps only textures. Autogen has 2 states. Forests have all 4, and their normals and weather masks vary too ([1000 world terrain/library.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/1000%20world%20terrain/library.txt); [1200 forests/library.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/1200%20forests/library.txt)). Winter textures contain **no painted snow**, so snow is always the weather pass's job ([textures10/hiveg](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/1000%20world%20terrain/textures10/hiveg/)). The GPU side confirms a continuous ratio. Vegetation points carry `season_rat_scale`, an assertion bounds `season_rat` to [0, 1], and the terrain shader binds `tex_seasonal_texture` and two seasonal composite masks ([vegetation_cull](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/shaders/bin/spv/vegetation_cull.xsa); [X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The executable also parses `.ter` directives `SEASONAL`, `SEASONAL_HILL`, `SEASONAL_CLIFF` and `SEASONAL_FAR` that no default terrain uses, so third-party terrain may rely on them ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). No season dataref exists ([DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)). The best-supported runtime model has three steps. Derive the current season and a 0–1 progress value from the tile's DSF rasters and the sim date. Resolve each virtual path against that season code (not one global art set). Then lerp each material's albedo from `TEXTURE` to `SEASONAL` by progress.

## Authoring rules a faithful renderer must honor

The public specs lag the content. The .ter (2018), .pol (2015) and .fac (2018) pages predate XP12, and the shipped 12.4 scenery leans on directives no page documents ([Current File Formats](https://developer.x-plane.com/docs/specs/)). A census of 9,848 .ter, 8,391 .pol, 8,149 .obj, 806 .fac, 497 .for and 437 .lin files sets the priorities ([default scenery](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/)):

| Directive | Shipped use | Documented? | Best-evidence meaning for SEDX |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NORMAL_METALNESS` | obj 4,349; fac 748; pol 340; lin 324; ter 114 | yes ([OBJ8](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/obj8-file-format-specification/)) | B = F0/metalness (0.08 split), A = gloss |
| `TEXTURE_MAP normal` / `material_gloss` | 12 OBJ lines | yes, garbled table | `_MAT`: R ≈ 10/255 (F0), G = gloss |
| `GLOBAL_specular` / `ATTR_shiny_rat` / `SPECULAR` | 6,105 / 70 / 1,067 | yes | scales normal-alpha gloss; OBJ default is 0, so normal-map gloss is invisible without it |
| `GLOBAL_luminance`, `ATTR_light_level … nits` | 403 / 266 OBJ lines | yes | LIT peak in nits, clamp 65,530 |
| `LUMINANCE`, `LIT_FADE_PARAMS`, `FAR` | 49 / 49 / 63 .ter | no | terrain LIT nits (600); far LIT fade; far-texture swap (for example `FAR 5000 20000 0.125`) |
| `SUPER_ROUGHNESS` | 0.5 ×14,117 (ter/pol); 1.0 ×942 (forests) | no | roughness override or floor; without it terrain renders too shiny |
| `WEATHER <mask>` | for 934; obj 234; pol 165; fac 105; lin 38; net 37 | no | per-material receptivity; `_WM` RGB (visually R damp/cracks, G puddles, B snow), `_SM` blue only |
| `WEATHER_TRANSPARENT` | obj 296; lin 99; pol 20, all on markings | no | markings likely inherit the surface's weather |
| `SNOW_ALBEDO_LUMA a b 0 0` | lin 209; obj 109; pol 56; fac 45 | no | feeds a snow-from-luminance mode; `0 0 0 0` disables (hangar interiors) |
| `SEASONAL <tex>` | 776 .for lines | no | next-season albedo for the progress blend |
| `BLEND_HASH 0.5`, `NORMAL_TRANSLUCENCY` | every 1200 forest shader | no | hashed alpha test; B = translucency with F0 = 0 |
| `RUNWAY_TILE` / `_NOISE_PARAMS` / `_MARKINGS` | pol 158 / 158 / 98 | no | runway tile shuffle and marking atlas |
| Decals and detailing | `DECAL_LIB` about 18,600 lines; `NORMAL_DECAL_PARAMS`; `TEXTURE_CONTROL` / `MODULATOR` / `TILE` | yes, 12.1+ for normal decals ([Standard Shading](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/standard-shading-options-for-x-plane-10-scenery/)) | two RGB/alpha decals and two normal decals keyed by `r g b a m k`; control map R/G/B/A = decal 1 / terrain layer / decal 2 / baked shadow |
| Shadow flags | `GLOBAL_no_shadow`, `ATTR_(no_)shadow`, `*_shadow_blend`, `NO_SHADOW` | yes | caster flags; alpha-tested shadow pass for blended materials |

The texture conventions matter as much as the directives. Two normal-map layouts coexist, and they must be chosen **per material flag, not per texture**. In the metalness layout (308 textures), RG holds the normal, B holds F0 and A holds gloss, and Z must be rebuilt. In the legacy layout (102 textures), B is Z and A is the specular level. Another 94 grayscale `_NML` files are specular-only, and 17 textures are used both ways ([default scenery](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/)). Terrain `NORMAL_TEX` maps have B = 0 even without the flag, so Z must always be rebuilt for terrain. Every albedo and LIT DDS is BC1 or BC3 without an sRGB flag. Suffixes therefore imply the colour space: `_ALB` and `_LIT` are sRGB, while `_NML`, `_WM`, `_SM`, `_CTL` and `_MOD` are linear. Parsers also need four fallbacks. **15,585 `.png` references resolve only to a same-named `.dds`**, and lights.txt points at a `.dds` that exists only as `.png`. 432 terrain files use decimal commas (`NORMAL_TEX 20,000000`). Paths mix `\` and `/`, and misspellings such as `NORMAL METALNESS` ship ([default scenery](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/default%20scenery/); [lights.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/bitmaps/world/lites/lights.txt)). Load-time conditionals (`GLOBAL_LIGHTING`, `GLOBAL_SHADOWS`, `SCENERY_SHADOWS`) reload files when render settings change ([OBJ8 spec](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/obj8-file-format-specification/)). Legacy content (XP9 `LIT_TEX`/`AUTO_VARY` terrain, 121 OBJ7 files) still ships for old DSFs.

HLSL sources (Hillaire, XeGTAO, SSSR, CACAO) port to Slang with minor binding changes. Wave-intrinsic code such as SPD maps onto Slang's `WaveActive*` operations under Vulkan 1.3 subgroups.

The build order should follow X-Plane's dependencies rather than visual payoff. The photometric spine goes first, because every later system is tuned against it: unit conventions, pre-exposed fp16, histogram EV100, AgX (or Neutral) and the CDL. Next comes the five-target G-buffer with the exact material decode and a self-generated height-correlated-Smith DFG table, plus probes. The Hillaire atmosphere and aerial perspective follow, then CSM and CACAO, then tiled candela lights driven by lights.txt. After those come the weather pass and the season-aware library resolver, which matter more to a scenery editor than anything cockpit-related. Then come the FFT water, then clouds with cloud shadows and froxel fog, and finally exposure fusion, bloom and SSR. FSR and DoF are optional for an editor.

## Fourteen open unknowns, each with a way to close it

Most remaining uncertainty is about **values**, not algorithms. X-Plane's own debug views and controls make most of it measurable without opening a shader. The screenshot tool exposes `photometric/ev100` and DoF as full datarefs, which allows locked-exposure A/B renders. `tonemap/false_color` gives luminance readouts, `gbuffer/debug_mode` offers "roughness", "light clipping" and "tile lights" views, and `twxr/debug_mode` and `debug_channel` ("none-mask-noise-coverage", "all-red-green-blue") isolate weather masks ([X-Plane.exe](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/X-Plane.exe)). The 12.2.0 beta 3 notes say safe-mode startup includes art controls to block plugin changes, so the measurements should come from a normal developer session ([fselite 12.2.0 b3](https://fselite.net/content/x-plane-12-2-0-beta-3-update-released/)).

## Laminar's shaders are a behavioral reference, never source

X-Plane's compiled shaders (`Resources/shaders/bin/spv/*.xsa` SPIR-V and `msl/*.xsa` Metal archives), the `*_mapping.xsv` binding maps, `Resources/brdf_lookup`, the executable, `lights.txt` and the default art are **Laminar Research's proprietary property**. This report used them only to learn observable behavior: pass names, resource formats, data flow, and a few input-output mappings such as the normal encoding and the gloss-to-roughness curve. SEDX's Slang shaders must be written from the published papers and the permissively licensed repositories above. That rules out transliterating decompiled SPIR-V or SPIRV-Cross output into Slang, lifting tables of constants (bloom tap gains, filter sample tables), and shipping `brdf_lookup`. Generate the DFG table with SEDX's own integrator. Any constant observed in Laminar's code (for example the 0.96875 roughness scale) should be re-derived and confirmed by black-box comparison against X-Plane's rendered output before use, so that SEDX's numbers come from measured behavior, not copied code. Where the team can manage it, a clean-room split helps: the people who read the binary catalog write specifications, and different people write shaders from those specifications plus public sources. Default textures, water-colour tiles, snow maps and lights.txt should be read at runtime from the user's own X-Plane install and never bundled. Laminar itself warns that `settings.txt` and `lights.txt` are internal formats, "completely unstable" and not for add-ons to depend on, so SEDX should treat art-control names as calibration hints, not an API ([settings.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/settings.txt); [lights.txt format](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/lights-txt-file-format/)). The research behind this report did not review Laminar's licence terms on reverse engineering. Check them before publishing any derived catalog outside the team.

## Conclusion

X-Plane 12's distinctive look comes much less from exotic technique than from calibration and authoring. Nearly every pass is a well-known published method. What makes an image read as "X-Plane" is a short list: physical units carried end to end, a hard EV100 floor, luminance-only exposure fusion, AgX plus an art-directed CDL, per-sky-condition ratios, and a weather pass that depends on per-material receptivity masks scenery authors paint. The planning consequence is that SEDX's critical path is a measurement campaign more than shader code. One DataRefTool dump of the art controls, a few RenderDoc captures and a synthetic test-scenery pack would turn most of this report's inferences into measured facts. X-Plane's lack of TAA and its scriptable fixed exposure make pixel-level A/B validation unusually practical.

The second takeaway concerns priorities. For a scenery editor, the authoring contract is the part of the renderer users will actually judge: metalness versus legacy normal maps chosen per material, `SUPER_ROUGHNESS`, `WEATHER` masks, `SNOW_ALBEDO_LUMA`, seasonal exports with `SEASONAL` blending, and candela lights. A renderer that gets those right with a merely good sky will look more like X-Plane than one with perfect clouds that renders terrain glossy or ignores seasons. The 12.2 recalibration is a warning too. Laminar retunes exposure, sky and clouds between releases, so SEDX should keep every X-Plane-derived value in versioned calibration data, not hard-coded constants, and pin a target build.
