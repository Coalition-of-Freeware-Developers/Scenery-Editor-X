## Q1. Which techniques did Laminar say it uses in each area, and what parameters or limits did it mention?

### Takeaway
X-Plane 12 runs only on Vulkan/Metal. It is a deferred, always-HDR (16-bit float), photometric renderer in cd/m² (nits). It has a physically based sky using a precomputed "froxel" scattering cache with Rayleigh, Mie, ozone and multiple-scattering terms; ray-marched volumetric clouds; AMD CACAO for SSAO; screen-space reflections plus filtered environment cube maps; an FFT ocean on a projected grid; FSR (spatial) upscaling with RCAS; and cascaded shadow maps. The tone mapper moved from a "custom ACES blend" to AgX in 12.2.0 (2025), and "exposure fusion" was added on top of auto-exposure. Laminar has never publicly named the atmosphere paper it built on. Cascade counts, the cube-map update rate and the auto-exposure algorithm are not in any Laminar statement found; they show up only in shipped settings and shader names.

### Cited Findings

**Backend architecture (Vulkan/Metal, deferred, MSAA, render graph)**
- Mar 4, 2022, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: deferred rendering is now the only mode, "real MSAA" works with it, FSR renders at lower resolution and upscales. X-Plane 12 itself does not use OpenGL; plugins still get OpenGL access. Staff comments: DLSS/TAA need motion vectors, which X-Plane lacks. Sidney Just said adding motion vectors conflicts with the existing "fat g-buffer". Ben noted the engine has a history buffer. FSR works with MSAA, does not need TAA, and works in VR. — [X-Plane 12 Development Update, March 4th 2022](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)
- Sep 2022 (page dated Sep 14, 2022), Ben Supnik [LR blog]: "X-Plane 12 uses Vulkan/Metal as its renderer, always." "virtually all new computing work in X-Plane 12 is on the GPU" (ocean waves moved from CPU to GPU). "X-Plane 12 has HDR always on." VRAM is reserved for "screen space reflections, 3-d water, dynamic weather effects, clouds". X-Plane does not use system memory as backup texture memory. In comments he said "OpenGL is only used for plugins." — [X-Plane 12 Early Access Is Here](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- Alpha 15 release note (alpha testing began Dec 3, 2021) [LR notes]: "Now requires Vulkan 1.3"; "Includes FSR & Cacao". Alpha 22: "Renamed FSR and Cacao 'Off' options to 'None'". — [X-Plane 12 Alpha Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes-alphas/)
- Jul 27, 2023, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: 12.06 "converted the main rendering pipeline from 12.0 (which was hand coded) into a rendering node graph". This lets VRAM be reused within a frame ("you can't double-book VRAM with OpenGL"), and the graph could later spread frame encoding across CPU cores. Sidney Just (comment): the graph reorders scene encoding, makes memory barriers static, needed zero shader changes, and X-Plane does not use AMD's Render Pipeline Shaders. — [X-Plane 12.06 Is Full of Many Things](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)
- 12.1.0 (RC Jun 20, 2024) MSAA changes [LR notes]: "FXAA is always enabled when MSAA is selected"; "Coverage to Alpha used on alpha-tested surfaces when MSAA is on"; "MSAA resolve is now scene referred for better visuals". Bug fixed: "Tone map vs MSAA resolve is backward." Beta 7 added an "Enable FXAA Antialiasing" checkbox. — [X-Plane 12.1.0 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/); RC date from [What Gets Released Before Vegas…](http://developer.x-plane.com/2024/06/what-gets-released-before-vegas-stays-released-in-vegas/)
- Oct 29, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog, comments]: the engine used FXAA and "now uses a 'single pass downsample (SPD) shader'". — [Free Form Friday: Lights, Water, Frost](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/)
- 12.4.0 (Dec 2025 to Feb 18, 2026) [LR notes]: "X-Plane now uses multiple CPU Cores to prepare scenery for rendering"; "Cockpit displays are excluded from FXAA processing"; "GPU selection support for Zink driver". — [X-Plane 12.4.0 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-0-release-notes/). Feb 24, 2026, Dellanie Byron [LR blog]: multi-threading "for scenery only"; GPU-limited systems are "unlikely to see any benefits". — [SDK News 12.4.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2026/02/sdk-news-12-4-0-fast-and-furious/)
- 12.4.4 betas (Sep 10 to Oct 6, 2026) [LR notes]: new Panel Graphics API that draws "without going through OpenGL, Cairo or NanoVG"; native Dear ImGui rendering; "Disabled async compute on macOS"; "Sparse residency for LIT textures" (opaque-black tiles not backed by memory); an on-disk texture cache; Zink updated to Mesa 25.3.4. — [X-Plane 12.4.4 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-4-release-notes/). FSExpo 2026 (Jun 12, 2026 report): the Panel Graphics API is "built directly on top of Vulkan". Laminar announced a new tile/layer "Tile Scenery Engine" with LODs and streaming that is "backwards compatible with DSFs", targeted for late 2026. — [Threshold, FSExpoFriday 2026](https://thresholdx.net/?p=43771) [Press]

**Photometric HDR, exposure, tone mapping**
- Nov 3, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: the renderer uses real-world luminance in cd/m² (nits). Reference values: building floodlights ~2 nt, old LDR monitor ~80, LCD ~500, clear sky ~7,000, clouds ~10,000, sunset sun ~600,000, noon sun ~1.6 billion. The pipeline is "entirely HDR, from start to finish" in 16-bit float. A simulated camera sets an exposure value; the art team sets exposure by time of day and weather; auto-exposure makes the camera more sensitive in dark areas and less in bright ones. A tone mapper compresses highlights for a film-camera look, with parameters tuned by art directors. — [Photometric Lighting – What is it and why do we need it?](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)
- Same post, Ben's comments [LR blog]: the pipeline stays sRGB (albedos authored in sRGB; wide gamut "doubtful" to matter). Highlight desaturation follows Timothy Lottes' "path to white" from his GDC tone-mapping talk, with an internal artist control for desaturation rate. HDR monitor output could come later, but a monitor's range is "still TINY compared to the HDR of the scene". VR doesn't fix single-exposure limits ("you'd need eye tracking"). UV/IR are not modelled because a spectral renderer is too costly. — [Photometric Lighting post](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)
- Jul 2023, Ben Supnik (comment) [LR blog]: X-Plane's minimum exposure is "an EV100 of 5". Real moonlight is too weak to register at that floor, so moonlight on the ground was not yet cast. — [X-Plane 12.06 Is Full of Many Things](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)
- 12.0 RC notes, late 2022 [LR notes]: RC1 "Refactor tone mapping from GPU blending to CPU readback" (XPD-13346). RC3: "The auto-exposure system is still being modified", with plans to "re-enable auto-exposure". RC4: "Cockpit is incorrectly exposed (autoexposure bug)" plus many per-aircraft "Lighting adjustments for ev5". RC6: "Further tuning of exposure and tonemapper settings". Beta 14: "Tone down color temperature changes". — [X-Plane 12.00 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes/)
- 12.2.0 Dev 1, Mar 7, 2025 [LR notes]: "Switched from our custom ACES blend to AgX, improving overall color accuracy, especially in bright and saturated scenes." Exposure fusion: "The simulator now balances dark and bright areas within the same frame, reducing harsh contrasts in cockpits". — [X-Plane 12.2.0 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)
- Apr 3, 2025, Marco Auer [LR news]: exposure fusion is described as the way a phone camera "takes multiple photos at various levels of exposure" and merges them. — [What's new in X-Plane 12.2.0?](https://www.x-plane.com/2025/04/whats-new-in-x-plane-12-2-0/)
- 12.3.0 Alpha 3, Aug 12, 2025 [LR notes]: "Better adaptive exposure fusion, especially for interior views, to avoid overly high contrast." Beta 1, Sep 4, 2025: fixed exposure fusion on AMD GPUs (XPD-17267). — [X-Plane 12.3.0 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-3-0-release-notes/)
- DataRefs.txt [Install]: `sim/graphics/misc/light_attenuation` says "Since v12 runs photometrically, no light source is _ever_ dimmed - the camera itself changes exposure, so this is always 1.0." A separate `light_attenuation_2d` exists for LDR 2-D panels. — [DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)
- [Install] X-Plane.exe contains the strings `exposure_fusion_graph`, `agX`, `agXStart`/`agXLast`, `cacao_build_cacao_graph` and "Cacao Controls", consistent with the release notes. — local binary string scan of X-Plane.exe (12.4.3)

**Bloom and glare**
- Feb 28, 2024, Daniela Rodríguez Careri (comment) [LR blog]: 12.1.0 bloom "will be noticeable on reflections", and "we tried to mimic the way the _eye_ perceives the bloom, not a camera." — [A Few Notes on 12.1.0 for Developers](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/02/a-few-notes-on-12-1-0-for-developers/)

**Atmosphere, sky and aerosols**
- Nov 3, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: sky luminance is computed from atmospheric composition, view angle and sun brightness. The same math works from any position, so the ground seen from the air picks up a matching blue tint. — [Photometric Lighting post](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)
- Oct 29, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: scattering for regular scenery is "normally pre-calculated in a special 'froxel cache'"; clouds also use it. The planet shader (rewritten by Daniel) computes far land and water scattering out to the horizon. For semi-transparent clouds Ben uses a weighted sum to "come up with one proxy atmo sample" per cloud, which breaks when density changes fast. — [Free Form Friday](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/)
- Sep 2022, Ben Supnik (comments) [LR blog]: the scattering model includes "an ozone absorbtion model"; at blue hour "the assumptions and simplifications of the scattering model that let it run on real time break down". — [Early Access Is Here](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- 12.2.0 Dev 1 [LR notes]: "Recalibrated Rayleigh scattering, ozone coefficients, Mie scattering, and multiple scattering for more natural sky colors"; "Clouds now cast visible shadows on the atmosphere"; "The moon now shifts color near the horizon due to atmospheric scattering"; sun and moon "Corrected size and brightness to match real-life values". Beta 2: "Visibility depiction is now improved. Hazy conditions are depicted more accurately". Known issue through Beta 1: "Sky Colors still require adjustments." — [12.2.0 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)
- 12.4.4 Beta 1, Sep 10, 2026 [LR notes]: "Visibility now fades from the ground-reported value to clear-air visibility over a physically derived altitude band", so fog, mist and haze stay in a shallower surface layer. Beta 2: "Fixed a seaming artifact in the sky dome". — [12.4.4 Release Notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-4-4-release-notes/)
- Mar 4, 2022, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: low visibility is calibrated with a runway "monolith" meant to be "just barely visible" at the set visibility, and runway lights respond to fog. — [Dev Update March 4 2022](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)
- Volumetric fog exists as a feature: 12.1.0 fixed a "Crash inside of render dag when volumetric fog is enabled", and 12.2.0 Beta 6 "Improved volumetric fog near the aircraft". — [12.1.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/); [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)
- DataRefs [Install]: `sim/graphics/settings/scattering_on` ("True if atmospheric scattering is enabled") and `sim/graphics/settings/HDR_on`. — [DataRefs.txt](file:///K:/SteamLibrary/steamapps/common/X-Plane%2012/Resources/plugins/DataRefs.txt)
- [Community] An MSFS-forum poster claims MSFS, DCS and X-Plane 12 all build on Bruneton's and Hillaire's papers, and that X-Plane 12 adopted a coefficient fix. This is unverified, and Laminar has not confirmed it. — [MSFS forum: ARPC atmosphere parameters](https://forums.flightsimulator.com/t/replace-the-atmosphere-parameters-with-more-accurate-ones-from-arpc/607603)

### Inferences
- The `scatter_compute_t` / `_multi` / `_skydome` / `_scattering` shader set, together with Laminar's "froxel cache" and "Rayleigh, ozone, Mie, multiple scattering" wording, fits a Bruneton-2008 or Hillaire-2020-style precomputed LUT atmosphere with a camera-volume (froxel) aerial-perspective LUT. Laminar has not named the paper.
- The FSR `lod_bias` values in settings.txt (-0.38, -0.58, -0.79, -1.0) match AMD's published mip-bias values for FSR 1.0's Ultra Quality / Quality / Balanced / Performance modes. With "no motion vectors" (2022), this points to spatial FSR 1 + RCAS, not FSR 2/3.
- `lumi_histo` + `histo_debug` shaders and the 12.0 RC1 note "tone mapping from GPU blending to CPU readback" suggest a GPU luminance histogram read back to the CPU to drive auto-exposure. Laminar has not described the algorithm.
- `ocean_spectrum` + `compute_fft` + `ocean_variance` suggest a spectrum-driven FFT ocean with a slope-variance term for distant filtering, similar to Bruneton et al. 2010 ocean lighting. The "proj grid" notes show a projected-grid ocean mesh.
- "Exposure fusion" as Laminar describes it (merging several exposures in one frame) matches the Mertens-style local tone mapping popularised for real time by Bart Wronski (2022, [blog](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)). Laminar has not cited it.
- Cascade counts depend on preset (1 to 4 exterior, 3 interior). This comes only from settings.txt, which Laminar warns is "completely unstable and subject to change".

### Gaps
- No Laminar statement found naming the atmosphere paper or model (Bruneton, Hillaire, or other), or giving aerosol/turbidity parameters beyond "recalibrated Mie".
- No public statement of the environment cube map update rate (only "sky-view update interval", "rebuilding environment maps" and "ambient light updates more often").
- No public description of SSR limits (resolution, ray steps, fallback) beyond bug notes.
- No public statement linking wave spectrum to wind speed (wind-driven sea state). The UI drives wave height, and wave length and speed follow from it.
- No Laminar statement on the auto-exposure algorithm (histogram percentiles, adaptation speed) or on how exposure fusion is weighted.
- The "light_tile"/"hybrid tiled lighting" design and the `svt` (virtual texturing?) shader were not described publicly.

## Q2. What changed in each 12.x release that affected the look? (version timeline)

## Q3. What did Laminar say about the intended look, and why scenes looked dark or washed out?

### Takeaway
Laminar's stated goal is to "render the world as it is": real-world luminance in nits, an always-on HDR pipeline, and a simulated camera with art-directed exposure by time and weather, plus auto-exposure and a film-like tone mapper. Laminar admitted taking artistic liberties for legibility. The long-running "dark cockpit" was explained in 2024 as correct lighting combined with an exposure gap between a bright exterior and a darker interior. A too-bright sky made it worse. It was fixed in 12.2.0 with exposure fusion, lower sky brightness, geometry-aware cockpit light, cloud-aware ambient and a separate interior ambient. Washed-out problems were mostly specific bugs (forests, far trees, G-buffer banding).

### Cited Findings
- Nov 3, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: the goal is to "render the world as it is". Because everything is calibrated, relative brightness (landing lights vs airport lights, taxi lights in sunlight) is set "mathematically" and can be checked against measurements. The art team favoured legibility and aesthetics in some places (Threshold's summary). — [Photometric Lighting](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/); [Threshold summary](https://thresholdx.net/lrskle/) [Press]
- Nov 2021, Ben (comments) [LR blog]: the exposure model simulates adaptation; real night adaptation needs flying full-screen in a dark room. Cockpit shadow contrast got "WORSE" because it is now real-world. — [Photometric Lighting](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)
- Sep 16, 2022, Ben Supnik (comment) [LR blog]: "I have a TODO to get better checking of the absolute light levels in the cockpit in the HDR buffer". A dark cockpit may be correct but needs verifying, and tone mapping depends on confirming the lighting first. The goal is solving "the perceptual problem with tone mapping" once internal light levels are physically right. — [Early Access Is Here](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- Jul 2023, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: dark cockpits are high priority, but cockpit light levels won't be retuned repeatedly. Third-party cockpit brightness hacks may need undoing when recalibration lands. Ben did not reply to a commenter who suggested exposure fusion or local tone mapping. — [12.06 post](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)
- Jun 2024, FSExpo, Laminar via fsnews [Press]: "the sim is properly rendering lighting", but "the difference in exposure between the very bright exterior and the relatively dark interior" caused dark cockpits; exposure fusion "exposes different parts of the image to different brightness". — [fsnews.eu](https://fsnews.eu/x-plane-12-to-receive-new-weather-radar-dark-cockpit-fix-and-more/)
- Apr 3, 2025, Marco Auer [LR news]: the sky was far brighter than necessary, clipping interior exposure to deep blacks and making bloom excessive. Sky exposure was lowered and ground object brightness corrected. Sunlight in the cockpit now follows the airframe's structure, which removes orange sunset tints. An ocean bug that darkened high-altitude cockpits was fixed. — [What's new in 12.2.0](https://www.x-plane.com/2025/04/whats-new-in-x-plane-12-2-0/)
- Apr 5, 2025, Dellanie Byron [LR blog]: with AgX "colors also desaturate under direct illumination" and "colors will shift in appearance. So please review your work." — [SDK News 12.2.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2025/04/12-2-0-chockingly-good-fun/)
- Washed-out fixes [LR notes]: "Far Tree Lighting is Washed Out" and "Fix for washed out forests" (12.0 betas 13/14); "Holodeck is washed out – not using real shading" (12.0 RC1); "Fixed washed-out colors and color banding in the G-buffer" (12.2.0 Alpha 2, Mar 21, 2025). — [12.00 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes/); [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)
- Exposure and brightness tuning after 12.2.0 [LR notes]: "Adjusted for brighter, more vibrant images across all times of day and weather conditions" (12.2.0 Dev 1); "Improved brightness when using external visuals" (12.3.0 Beta 4). — [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/); [12.3.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-3-0-release-notes/)
- [Community] Tom Knudsen (comment, Oct 2021) argued users misjudge night lighting because of white balance and misread light.txt values. — [Free Form Friday comments](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/). [Community] Third-party "dark cockpit fix" guides and lighting mods were widespread before 12.2.0. — [X-Crafts guide](https://www.xcrafts.com/how-to-fix-dark-cockpit-in-xplane-12)

### Inferences
- Laminar's position stayed the same from 2021 to 2025: the lighting is physically right, and the camera model (single global exposure plus tone mapper) is the perceptual weak point. Exposure fusion is the answer to the single-exposure limit Ben described in 2021 ("you'd need eye tracking").
- The EV100 = 5 floor (2023) explains why moonlit scenes stay dark and why moonlight on the ground was deferred.

### Gaps
- No Laminar statement explains the specific early-beta exposure targets (EV values by time of day) or the art-direction curves.
- No public before/after luminance measurements of cockpit interiors were found.

## Q4. How does X-Plane drive weather surface effects and seasons, and which datarefs expose them?

### Takeaway
Wet pavement, puddles, snow and ice come from a GPU "weather surface shader" (Petr and Sidney, 2021) applied to scenery and roads. The weather system drives it through a regional runway-condition enum and snow-cover ratio, with precipitation type set by temperature. Before 12.4.4, ground snow came from weather settings. 12.4.4 (Sep 2026, beta) makes snow coverage GRIB-driven, persistent and regional. Seasons, per 2021 Laminar statements, are continuous shader interpolation by date and latitude using climate and biome data, with bare winter trees. No public season dataref exists. Laminar has not documented how rain wetness builds up or dries over time.

### Cited Findings
- Oct 29, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: "Petr and Sidney" built "the weather surface shader", which adds water to surfaces and makes pavement wet. Shading changes "once the water forms a real puddle", and rough wet materials' "reflections change with angle". — [Free Form Friday](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/)
- FSExpo 2021 [Press]: "volumetric puddles and snow" as weather ground effects; librain integrated for rain on aircraft. — [Threshold](https://thresholdx.net/xp12fs/)
- Mar 4, 2022 [LR blog]: task list includes "Rain/Snow on Runways", "Rain Improvements", "Seasons", "3D Trees". — [Dev Update](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/)
- [Press, preview] Threshold's 2022 preview summary reported that ice turns to puddles as temperatures rise and puddles freeze below zero. This is preview language, not documentation. — [Threshold X-Plane 12 Previews summary](https://thresholdx.net/xp12pv/) (seen via search snippet only)
- Laminar manual: precipitation falls as rain, hail or snow "Depending on the temperature around the airplane" and where it formed. — [X-Plane desktop manual](https://www.x-plane.com/manuals/desktop/)
- Alpha notes [LR notes]: "Snow suddenly appears / disappears" (A36/A37); "Fixed level for 'no snow' to avoid traces of snow" (A24); "Auto-normalize luma in snow" (A3); "Wet pavement throughout replay" (A31); "Impossible to set up overcast skies without pouring rain" (A38). — [Alpha notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes-alphas/)

OBJ8 adds `RAIN_scale`, `RAIN_friction` (12.1), `THERMAL_texture`, `THERMAL_source`/`THERMAL_source2` (12.1), `WIPER_texture` and `WIPER_param`. 12.1.0 icing is "based on the surface normal and wind direction". — [Free Form Friday](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/10/free-form-friday-lights-water-frost/); [OBJ8 spec](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/obj8-file-format-specification/); [12.1.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-1-0-release-notes/)

### Inferences
- `runway_friction` folds wetness, puddles, snow and ice into one 0 to 15 enum (with intensity sub-steps). This is probably the main state the surface shader and the friction model share, alongside `snow_cover`.
- The shipped shader names `weather_apply_compute`/`_raster`, `rain_surface`, `rain_ice_accumulation`, `ripple` and `rain_normals` point to a GPU pass that builds wetness, puddle, ripple and snow masks and applies them to G-buffer materials. Laminar has not described these passes.
- Seasons look date- and latitude-driven (continuous), not temperature-driven. Snow was weather-driven (manual or METAR) until 12.4.4 added GRIB snow cover.

### Gaps
- No Laminar statement found on rain wetness accumulation over time, drying after rain stops, or whether METAR remarks (runway condition codes) drive contamination.
- No public documentation of the climate and biome dataset, or the formula for season state. No author-facing seasonal art API documented in the sources reached.
- Whether temperature affects seasons, or only snow and icing, is unconfirmed.

## Q5. What did Laminar tell third-party authors about how content should look?

### Takeaway
Laminar asks authors to use real-world photometric values: nits for LIT textures and displays, FAA spec intensities for lights. Albedo and emissive changes should be made in textures, not material hacks. After 12.2.0, authors were told to review colours under AgX, remove cockpit-brightening hacks and moved cube maps, review albedos, update terrain roughness and normal direction, re-check glass and shiny materials, and remove private art controls. No numeric albedo or roughness ranges were published in the sources reached.

### Cited Findings
- Nov 3, 2021, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: authors enter real-world brightness values, for example a glass display's spec instead of eyeballing (Threshold's example: a 500-nit LCD instead of a "0.954" fudge value). Scenery developers "can customize all aspects of the scenery lights". — [Photometric Lighting](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/); [Threshold](https://thresholdx.net/lrskle/) [Press]
- OBJ8 spec [LR docs]: `GLOBAL_luminance <nts>` "Sets the nits value for the lit texture" (a duplicate entry says the baseline LIT luminance "is clamped at 65530"). `ATTR_light_level` with a brightness in nits enables photometric features. `TEXTURE_MAP <usage>` (normal, material_gloss, gloss) was added for v12. Normal-map alpha shininess is scaled by `ATTR_shiny_rat`/`GLOBAL_specular` (default 0). `NORMAL_METALNESS` puts base reflectance in blue. Cockpit devices are "not subject to" light-level attributes. Guidance: "It is strongly recommended that albedo and emissive color changes be made using the day and lit textures." — [OBJ8 File Format Specification](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/obj8-file-format-specification/)
- 12.0 RC3 note (late 2022) [LR notes]: RC3 changes how brightness is managed in glass displays, and authors should enter the real max luminance. — [12.00 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-00-release-notes/)
- Nov 2021, Ben (comment) [LR blog]: albedos are authored in sRGB; the move from painted photo textures to per-channel material properties has been ongoing since XP11. — [Photometric Lighting](https://developer.x-plane.com/2021/11/photometric-lighting-what-is-it-and-why-do-we-need-it/)
- Jul 2023, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: third parties who "fix" cockpit brightness themselves may have to undo it after recalibration. — [12.06 post](http://developer.x-plane.com/2023/07/x-plane-12-06-is-full-of-many-things/)
- Apr 5, 2025, Dellanie Byron, SDK News 12.2.0 [LR blog]: the AgX tonemapper "significantly improved the range of colors", but "colors will shift in appearance. So please review your work" (artists, livery painters, scenery designers). Developers who moved cube map positions to fix dark cockpits should "return them to their default position". Texture artists should "review albedos, including attempts to brighten cockpits". Generated terrain content "will need to be updated with proper roughness and normal direction". "Atmospheric/Cloud datarefs will behave significantly differently". Users of private art controls "will need to review/remove them from their product". — [SDK News 12.2.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2025/04/12-2-0-chockingly-good-fun/)
- 12.2.0 Dev 1 [LR notes]: "Glass and shiny materials may need slight adjustments in 3D models." Beta 7: "Improved lighting of emissive textures." — [12.2.0 notes](https://www.x-plane.com/kb/x-plane-12-2-0-release-notes/)
- Feb 24, 2024, Ben Supnik [LR blog]: 12.1.0 normal-map decals give "better looking materials at different lighting angles"; decals work in aircraft OBJs; `LOAD_CENTER` now works on OBJs. — [A Few Notes on 12.1.0](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/02/a-few-notes-on-12-1-0-for-developers/)

### Inferences
- For a scenery editor that previews X-Plane 12 content, the authoring contract is: sRGB albedo textures, a separate LIT texture with a nit baseline (`GLOBAL_luminance`, clamp 65,530), normal-map metalness (blue) and shininess (alpha) scaled by `GLOBAL_specular`/`ATTR_shiny_rat`, and lights at real candela. Exposure and tone mapping are the sim's job.
- Laminar has repeatedly discouraged brightness hacks (2023, 2025). Content tuned to look right under pre-12.2 exposure may now look too bright.

### Gaps
- No published recommended albedo ranges (for example, linear reflectance bounds for terrain or buildings), roughness ranges, or emissive nit targets for common objects (signs, windows, apron floods) were found.
- The "Standard Shading Options" document referenced for decals was not reviewed.
- No author guidance on seasonal variants or weather-surface-effect opt-outs (no `ATTR_no_weather`-style directive appears in the OBJ8 spec text retrieved).
